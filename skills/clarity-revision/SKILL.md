# Clarity Revision

Revise unclear prose by aligning characters with subjects and actions with verbs.

## Principles

1. Make the main characters the grammatical subjects.
2. Express important actions as verbs rather than abstract nouns.
3. Preserve the writer's meaning, emphasis, and factual claims.

## Process

Use three steps: **analyze, assess, rewrite**.

### 1. Analyze

For each sentence:

1. Ignore a short introductory phrase of four or five words.
2. Inspect the first seven or eight words.
3. Identify the grammatical subject and the first main verb.
4. Mark abstract nouns in the subject position.
5. Count how many words the reader crosses before reaching a verb.

Flag a sentence for likely revision when either condition is true:

- An abstract noun serves as the simple subject.
- Seven or eight words pass before the reader reaches a verb.

Treat these as diagnostic signals, not mechanical errors.

### 2. Assess

Ask:

1. Who or what are the sentence's main characters?
2. What actions do those characters perform?
3. Are important actions hidden in **nominalizations**: abstract nouns derived from verbs?
4. Does the current subject name a main character?
5. Do the main verbs express the important actions?

Prefer concrete actors when the sentence names them. Do not invent an actor the source does not support.

### 3. Rewrite

1. Turn nominalized actions back into verbs: **automation → automate**, **loss → lose**.
2. Make the characters the subjects of those verbs: **corporations automate**, **blue-collar workers lose**.
3. Use subordinating conjunctions such as **because, if, when, although, why, how, whether,** or **that** to state the relationship among ideas.
4. Move secondary details after the subject and verb where possible.
5. Keep necessary qualifications, relationships, and emphasis.
6. Read the revision for natural rhythm; do not force every sentence into the same shape.

## Example

Before:

> The automation of manufacturing, assembly, and shipping processes by corporations means the loss of jobs for many blue-collar workers.

Analysis:

- Abstract subject: **automation**
- First main verb: **means**, reached after ten words
- Main characters: **corporations**, **blue-collar workers**
- Important actions: **automate**, **lose jobs**

After:

> Many blue-collar workers are losing their jobs because corporations are automating their manufacturing, assembly, and shipping processes.

## Output

When revising a passage, return:

1. The revised prose.
2. A short note listing the main character/action changes, only when useful or requested.

Do not burden the user with sentence-by-sentence diagnostics unless they ask for them.

## Final Check

- Do main subjects name the people or things the passage is about?
- Do main verbs carry the important actions instead of nominalizations?
- Does a conjunction make the relationship among ideas explicit when needed?
- Can the reader reach the subject and verb without crossing a long abstract phrase?
- Did the revision preserve the original meaning?
- Does the prose still sound natural rather than mechanically simplified?
