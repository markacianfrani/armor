# Wholeness

Some things have more life in them than others, not because we like them but
because of how they are made: the parts support one another, there is a center the
thing is really about, and nothing is forced. We feel it before we can explain it.
That feeling is the instrument, and we are trained to distrust it.

## The question

Put it to each option in turn, and answer from the gut:

- Which one is the more faithful representation of my self?
- If I had to live inside one of them, which would I be more comfortable
  inhabiting?
- Which has more life in it — which feels settled, and which feels forced?

Project an honest self, weaknesses included, not the version we would prefer to
present. The self we wish we were picks the impressive option.

## The procedure

1. **Connect first.** Stay with each option until it can be held as one thing — a
   minute or two, not a glance. Meet it as something with a presence of its own,
   not as dead matter to rule on.
2. **Divert the ego.** Cleverer, newer, prouder to sign, expected by the team:
   none of that is the question.
3. **Ask slowly**, one and then the other, and let the body answer before the mind
   explains.
4. **Check convergence.** Ask another person, alone, with no pre-argument.
   Agreement means we are reading something real. A scatter means neither is more
   alive, or an ego answered.
5. **Then look for reasons** — but do not let a clever one talk us out of what we
   felt.

Never steer the feedback towards what we already wanted. Close calls are normal; a
near tie is not a failure.

## Reading life

Life is relative here: more alive than the other, not alive or dead.

**More alive.** Parts strengthen one another. A strong center, one evident thing
it is about. Detail at every scale. Settled, with nothing struggling to get out.
It could grow without breaking. Nothing arbitrary.

**Less alive.** Parts ignore or fight one another. No center, so everything is
equally important. Bent to fit, and we can see where. Ornament tacked on rather
than grown out of the form. Brittle. Arbitrary.

## Guards

Keep the practical constraints in place — the spec, the budget, the patterns this
codebase already repeats. Without them the felt sense wanders into excursions that
seem right and work badly. We are not making fine art.

Skip the test when the facts decide: one option wrong, faster by a margin that
matters, or required by a constraint. Skip it for small reversible choices. And if
an option repels us on sight, no study afterwards redeems it — that is already the
answer.

Apply it at every fork, not once at the end. Each use trains the sense.
